

 
                 THE BOY WHO WANTED TO FLY
 
                             STORY BY JASON WHITEWOLF
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
SCENE 1:
 
 
 
Orion was a young boy with a big dream: to learn how to fly. Ever since he was very little, He had dreamed of soaring through the sky, and this summer had been no exception. One Saturday, Orion had a thought. "If I'm going to fly, I need to make some wings!" So, he went to the garage and searched for materials. He found some empty cardboard boxes and grabbed a pair of scissors and some tape from the workbench. He spent a long time in the garage, cutting and shaping the cardboard into wing shapes. He used the tape to secure the wings to his arms, making sure they wouldn't fall off.
 
 
 
SCENE 2:
 
 
 
Finally, his wings were ready. He walked out to the front yard, put on his wings, and started running around, flapping his arms as hard as he could. But no matter how fast he ran, or how high he jumped, he couldn't get off the ground. He flapped his arms as hard as he could and ran as fast as he could, but he still could not fly. After a long time running around his front yard, flapping his arms and jumping up in the air, Orion got tired and sat down on the grass to rest. The golden sun was slowly starting to set, as it gently dipped below the horizon. The leaves on the trees rustled in the cool, autumn breeze. The crisp, cool air was filled with the scent of falling leaves, wood smoke, and the sweet aroma of ripe apples and pumpkins. Orion could hear his mom calling him in for dinner. He took off his wings and put them in the garage, then went inside to eat his favorite meal: spaghetti with meatballs.
 
 
 
 
SCENE 3:
 
 
 
 
After dinner, Orion helped his mom and dad clean up and wash the dishes. Then, He headed up to his bedroom to get ready for bed. As he walked up the stairs, he couldn't help but feel a sense of excitement and anticipation. His bedroom was his own personal retreat, a place where he could relax and be himself.
 
As he entered the room, he was greeted by the sight of his walls, which were covered in pictures of airplanes, birds, and superheroes, all of them flying through the air. Orion loved these images and spent a few moments gazing at them, letting his mind wander and imagining what it would be like to fly.
 
Finally, after changing into his pajamas and brushing his teeth, He climbed into bed and snuggled under the covers. As he closed his eyes, he couldn't help but feel grateful for the warm, comfortable bed and the roof over his head. He fell asleep with a smile on his face.
 
 
 
 
SCENE 4:
 
 
 
 
The next morning, Orion woke up with a new idea: "What if I put feathers on my cardboard wings?" he thought to himself. He was so excited about this new idea that he jumped out of bed and got dressed quickly. He started running down the stairs, thinking to himself, "This just might work!"
 
"No running down the stairs!" his mom called out from the kitchen. This wasn't the first time she had reminded him of this rule.
 
"Yes, Mom," Orion replied, slowing down as he walked the rest of the way down. "Eat your blueberry pancakes before you go out to play," his mom said as he sat down at the table.
 
"Oh man," Orion groaned, because he really just wanted to try out his new idea. But he eagerly finished his breakfast and asked his mom if he could go outside to play. His mom gave him a big smile and said, "Of course you can."
 
He leapt from the table with so much excitement that he almost knocked over his chair. He quietly put the chair back in place and hurried out to the garage to get his cardboard wings.
 
 
 
 
SCENE 5:
 
 
 
 
After getting his wings and tape, He gathered feathers from the chicken coop, trying his best not to disturb the chickens who were busy clucking and pecking about. As he searched for the perfect feathers, he couldn't help but laugh at the silly birds. One particularly mischievous chicken kept trying to sneak up and peck at his shoes, no doubt hoping for a tasty treat. Orion managed to gather a good supply of feathers and brought them back to the house. He used tape to attach them to his homemade wings and was so excited to test them out that he barely even noticed the chicken that had snuck up and was now tugging at the hem of his shirt.
 
"This should work!" he thought to himself. He put on his feathery wings and ran out to the front yard.
 
 
 
 
 
 
SCENE 6:
 
 
 
 
 
"Here goes nothing!" said Orion as he took off running at top speed, flapping his arms as hard as he could. At first, he struggled to get off the ground, but he refused to give up. He kept running and flapping, determined to fly. He continued to push himself, panting and sweating as he ran.
 
Eventually, he got tired and was forced to take a break. He collapsed onto the grass, feeling a mix of frustration and determination. As he sat there, he couldn't help but wonder what he was doing wrong. Then, a sudden spark of inspiration hit him. "Maybe I just need to jump higher!"
 
Acting quickly on his idea, Orion sprang to his feet and ran back to the garage. He rummaged through the clutter until he found a ladder and a rake. He grabbed both items and hurried back to the front yard.
 
 
 
 
 
 
SCENE 7:
 
 
 
 
It was late autumn, and the trees in Orion's yard were ablaze with color. The leaves, which had once been a vibrant green, had now turned a multitude of shades including red, orange, pink, yellow, and brown. Most of the leaves had already fallen to the ground, creating a carpet of color across the lawn.
 
He set to work raking up the leaves into a big pile. He used the rake to gather the leaves together, forming a large and fluffy heap.
 
Once the pile was complete, he placed the ladder next to it. The ladder was very tall, and Orion really liked this idea. He climbed to the very top of the ladder, started flapping his new wings, and then, with all his might, he jumped off, hoping to soar through the air. Orion didn't fly at all; instead of flying, he fell right into the big pile of leaves, causing them to fly up into the air around him. Even though he couldn't fly, it was still a lot of fun, so laughing and tumbling, he tried it again and again.
 
 
 
SCENE 8:
 
 
 
 
"A short time later, as he was happily playing in the leaves, a garbage truck came rumbling down the street and stopped in front of his house. The driver, a tall and burly man with a rough, stubbly face and large shoes, emerged from the truck and walked towards Orion. Despite his intimidating appearance, Orion could see kindness in the man's eyes as he knelt down to speak to him.
 
"'Do you really want to learn to fly?' the man asked with a gentle tone.
 
"'More than anything,' Orion replied with a determined look on his face.
 
"The man chuckled. 'If you really want to fly, then you can fly in your dreams,' he said with a smile. 'How do I do that?' asked Orion, his eyes wide with curiosity.
 
"'Practice, my boy, practice,' the man replied. 'Just keep practicing and never give up on your dream.'
 
"With a smile and a wave, the man returned to his truck and drove away, leaving him to lie in the leaves and ponder his words. As he gazed up at the sky, he made a promise to himself to never give up on his dream of flying and to practice every chance he got."
 
 
 
 
SCENE 9:
 
 
 
 
 
"That night, Orion climbed into his cozy bed, nestled in the corner of his room. The blankets were soft and thick, made of a plush fabric that always made him feel warm and secure. He pulled them up to his chin and closed his eyes, feeling the comforting darkness envelope him.
 
"As he lay there, he couldn't help but wonder if he would be able to fly in his dreams. He had always loved the idea of soaring through the air, and he hoped that perhaps tonight he would be able to experience it for himself.
 
"A short while later, Orion's parents came into his room, their footsteps muffled by the plush carpet. His mother leaned down to kiss him on the forehead, her long hair tickling his face. 'Hey dad,' said Orion, 'Do you think I can fly in my dreams?'
 
"Orion's dad smiled, and simply said, 'I think you can.' This made him very happy. Then he nodded and snuggled deeper into his blankets, already feeling the weightless sensation of flight in his mind. He closed his eyes and let out a contented sigh, and before he knew it, he had drifted off into a deep and peaceful sleep, filled with wonderful dreams."
 
 
 
 
 
SCENE 10:
 
 
 
 
"As he drifted off to sleep, he could hear the howling wind outside, and the tapping of a branch on his window...tap...tap...tap... As the noise got louder and louder, it almost woke him up, but he did not want to wake up because he was having a really good dream. Then he remembered that he should try to fly in his dreams. So in his dream, he started flapping his arms and tried to run super-fast so he could fly, but when he tried, it felt like he was running through honey.
 
"As he ran through the thick, sticky honey, he struggled to keep his footing. The heavy, gooey substance slowed his progress, making each step a struggle as he fought against the suction of the honey. His feet felt like they were stuck to the ground, his legs heavy and sluggish. Frustrated, he then dreamed about his friends and the swimming pool they used to visit every summer. Remembering the sound of laughter and splashing as they played and swam. That was a very nice dream."
 
 
 
 
SCENE 11:
 
 
 
 
"As he lay in bed the next morning, Orion's mind was still racing with excitement from the previous night's dream. He remembered how hard it was to run in his dream. It was so hard that he could not even jump! He could vividly recall every detail, from the feeling of the cool night air, to the way his heart seemed to pulse with the power of flight. He thought about this for a while until he got a new idea. 'Maybe if I wear my Superman cape, it will help me fly in my sleep?'
 
"So that night, before he went to bed, he put on his Superman cape, and then he fell asleep."
 
 
 
 
SCENE 12:
 
 
 
 
A few hours later, Orion found himself back in the dream world. He was determined to try to fly again. He knew that this was his chance to make it happen. This time, he had on his Superman cape. The cape was made of a shiny, red fabric that flowed behind him as he moved. He had always loved the way it made him feel - like a superhero, ready to take on any challenge that came his way. He really believed he could do it this time. So, in his dream, he jumped into the air as hard as he could! Then suddenly, a very amazing thing happened. He started to fly! It felt amazing to him. He was floating in the air, like a bubble. But when he tried to control his flying, he once again had a hard time.
 
 
 
 
SCENE 13:
 
 
 
 
Orion spread his arms out in front of him, just like Superman, and for a moment, it seemed to work. He felt a little more in control, as if he were steering himself through the air. "Maybe Superman flies with his hands?" he thought to himself, feeling a glimmer of hope. He focused on his hands, trying to will them to move him in the direction he wanted to go.
 
But as he tried to fly using his hands, he found that it was a lot harder than he had anticipated. His movements felt wobbly and uncertain, as if he were trying to balance on a tightrope. Frustrated, he decided to try a different approach. "Maybe Superman flies with his thumbs?" He extended his thumbs and tried to use them to steer himself through the air.
 
But to his surprise, he found that this was even more difficult than using his hands. Every time he moved his thumbs, even just a little bit, his body would abruptly and sharply fly in that direction. "Well, that's not going to work!" he thought to himself, feeling a little defeated. It seemed that flying in his dreams was a lot harder than he had imagined. But even so, he wasn't ready to give up just yet. 
 
 
 
 
 
SCENE 14:
 
 
 
 
 
Then he thought, “Maybe Superman can fly so well using the letter “S" on his chest." So, Orion stuck out his chest and began to fly. Amazingly something was very different this time. He could control his flying. The further he pushed out his chest, the faster he would fly. When he pulled his chest back, he could easily slow down. Then, when he moved his chest to the left and the right, he would fly that way too!
 
But then, Orion thought to himself, "I don’t have an “S" on my chest, like Superman." "So how can I do it?" "Maybe it’s my heart that makes me fly!"
 
 
 
SCENE 15:
 
 
 
 
Orion’s heart was so full of love for flying that it made his heart very powerful. So, he opened his heart all the way, as much as he could. He let all his love for flying guide him. He flew higher and higher, laughing with joy as the wind rushed past him. It was the most amazing feeling he had ever experienced. He flew all around the neighborhood, taking in the sights and sounds of the autumn day. He flew over houses, trees, through the clouds, and over the moon. He flew higher and higher, until he was soaring through the starry sky. It was the most incredible adventure of his life. Then he began to fly faster and faster and faster!
 
 
 
 
 
SCENE 16:
 
 
 
Orion flew so incredibly fast in his dreams that night that he lost his shoes! His shoes came right off his feet because his shoelaces were not tied on tight enough. And that is why, if you ever see a pair of shoes hanging from a telephone wire, they were lost by kids who were flying in their dreams.
 
 
 
 
SCENE 17:
 
 
 
From that day on, Orion was known as the boy who could fly. He flew in his dreams all the time, exploring the world, and having adventures. And every time he flew, he remembered the long journey it had taken him to get there. But it was all worth it, because he had finally achieved his dream of learning how to fly.
 
 
 
 
The end.
